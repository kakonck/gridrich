3Grid

Projection and extent
Grids are built in a Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection centred on the boundary, so every cell has the same true area. Boundary and records are converted to it automatically. The projection is intended for local to continental study areas (up to a few thousand kilometres across); shape distortion grows with distance from the centre, and areas crossing the antimeridian are not supported. Only records that fall inside the boundary outline are assigned to cells.

Layers:

All selected grids drawn side by side with the map's palette, classes, basemap and empty-cell settings. A shared colour scale uses the range of all maps together, so the same colour means the same value in every panel. Hover a cell for its values.

Each chart has its own PNG and SVG buttons; the zip buttons save every chart of the selected grid at once. Images have a white background and the title embedded, ready for a manuscript or presentation.

Pairwise indices from the presence/absence matrix: a = species shared, b = species only in the first unit, c = only in the second. Jaccard = a / (a + b + c); Sorensen = 2a / (2a + b + c); Sorensen dissimilarity = (b + c) / (2a + b + c); Simpson dissimilarity (turnover) = min(b, c) / (a + min(b, c)); nestedness component = Sorensen dissimilarity minus turnover (Baselga 2010). The tiles give Whittaker's beta (gamma / mean alpha - 1) and the multiple-site Sorensen partition across all occupied units. Units without records are excluded. Pairwise matrices are computed for up to 1,500 units.

How to use Gridrich

1. Boundary. Load the outline of the study area. Accepted: GeoJSON (.geojson or .json), KML, a zipped shapefile (.zip; if the archive holds several layers, such as GADM levels 0 to 3, choose the layer to use), or the .shp, .dbf and .prj files selected together. Several polygons are merged into one outline; holes are kept. Very detailed files (more than 40,000 vertices) are kept as separate polygons instead of being merged. Features that overlap each other are rejected, because overlapping polygons would count area and records twice: dissolve them or fix the shared borders in a GIS first. Self-intersecting polygons are rejected as well, unless you tick the repair option, in which case they are split into simple parts and the log says so. GeoJSON and KML are expected in longitude/latitude; a shapefile is reprojected from its .prj file; without a .prj file, or when the coordinates are projected, you must confirm or choose the coordinate system before the boundary is accepted. Study areas that cross the antimeridian or span more than 180 degrees of longitude are not supported, and areas more than 5,000 km across get a warning about shape distortion. GeoPackage is not supported in the browser; export the layer as GeoJSON from QGIS or ArcGIS first.

2. Records. Load a table with one row per observation: CSV, TSV, TXT or Excel, or a GBIF download zip (the table inside is read directly). Columns for species, longitude and latitude are detected from common names (GBIF: species, decimalLongitude, decimalLatitude; iNaturalist: scientific_name, longitude, latitude) and can be changed in the drop-downs. Only the first sheet of a workbook is read. Coordinates are assumed to be WGS84 longitude/latitude unless you choose UTM or give a PROJ string; if most values are far outside the longitude/latitude range the app switches to UTM and asks you to set the zone, and if records only fit the boundary when longitude and latitude are exchanged it tells you the columns are probably swapped. Rows without coordinates or species are dropped and counted in the log. Under "Optional screening columns" you can point to a taxon rank column (GBIF: taxonRank, detected automatically) so that records above species level are dropped and counted, and to a coordinate uncertainty column (GBIF: coordinateUncertaintyInMeters) with an optional maximum; after the run the summary reports how many assigned records have an uncertainty larger than each cell size. Without a rank column, names above species level are counted as they are and the data-quality table says the rank was not checked. Species names are trimmed and internal runs of spaces collapsed, but otherwise matched exactly (case sensitive), so standardise spelling and synonyms first. Only records that fall inside the boundary outline are analysed; records outside it, or in holes, are counted and excluded.

3. Grid. Enter one or more cell sizes in kilometres. "Suggest sizes" proposes three sizes that give roughly 400, 100 and 25 cells for the boundary area (rounded to convenient values). Squares use the side length; hexagons use the distance between opposite edges. The minimum share inside the boundary is 0 by default: every cell that touches the study area is kept, so every record inside the outline is assigned at every cell size and gamma richness does not depend on the cell size. Raising it (0.5 is a common choice; 1 keeps only cells completely inside) removes edge cells that are mostly outside, and the records in them are then reported as records_in_removed_cells; use area_in_km2 or prop_in afterwards to judge small edge cells rather than discarding their records. Clipping cuts edge cells to the outline. Rarefied richness is the expected number of species in a random sample of a fixed number of records (at least 2) drawn from the cell (Hurlbert 1971); it standardises the number of records compared and reduces, but does not remove, the effect of unequal sampling intensity. Cells with fewer records get no value.

4. Results. Each cell carries: cell_id, cellsize, cell_area_km2, area_in_km2, prop_in, x and y (cell centre in the grid projection, metres), lon and lat (cell centre, WGS84), n_occ (records), richness (distinct species), occupied, and optionally species_list and richness_rarefied. Records on a shared edge are counted in one cell only, so the total equals the number of records assigned to that grid. Records inside the boundary that fall in edge cells removed by the minimum share are counted separately (records_in_removed_cells) for every cell size, so the audit trail is complete: submitted, dropped for coordinates, rank, uncertainty or name, outside the boundary, in removed cells, assigned.

Metrics. For each cell i: occurrence count O_i = number of records assigned to the cell; observed species richness S_i = number of distinct species with at least one record; occurrence-record density = O_i / A_i where A_i is the cell area inside the boundary (records per km2), a proxy for sampling intensity rather than a direct measure of effort; percentage of observed species represented = S_i / S_total x 100 where S_total is the species pool retained at that cell size (so the value is not comparable across cell sizes); species frequency F_j = number of cells in which species j occurs (Species tab); rarefied richness = expected species in a random sample of n records (Hurlbert 1971), not calculated for cells with fewer than n records. Summary means and medians are over occupied cells only (mean_richness_occupied, mean_occ_occupied), and gamma richness is the total observed richness across the cells of a grid, not an estimate of true regional diversity. Coordinate duplicates (same species name and coordinates; date, observer and identifiers are not compared, so they may be separate legitimate observations) are counted and reported; they raise O_i but never S_i, and can be removed with the option in step 3. The presence/absence matrix PA_ij (1 if species j occurs in cell i) is available in the Export tab.

Charts tab. Six charts read the results: richness against sampling effort, the distribution of richness, the effect of cell size, the most widespread species, and richness and effort by region, each with a plain-language reading. Options: colour bars and dots by value with any map palette or use a single colour; colour the effort-chart dots by rarefied richness, share inside the boundary or record density; linear or logarithmic axes for the effort chart; number of species shown; histogram bins; value labels. Every chart has PNG and SVG buttons; the toolbar saves all charts of the selected grid as a zip, at the PNG width you choose (3000 px by default, about 300 dpi for a full-width figure). Exports have a white background and the title embedded.

Compare maps tab. Draws every grid, and the regions, side by side for one variable (or one species), using the map's palette, classes, basemap and empty-cell settings. With a shared colour scale the same colour means the same value in every panel, which is the right setting for judging the effect of cell size; with separate scales each panel uses its own range. Choose which layers and how many columns to show, hover a cell for its values, and save the whole panel as PNG (width adjustable) or SVG.

Methods tab. A methods statement written from your actual settings and results, a reproducibility table, a JSON configuration file and a complete HTML report (methods, data quality, summaries, species, map) for the supplementary material of a paper. Gridrich reports observed richness in the analysed dataset; it does not claim to measure true biodiversity.

Map overlays. "Show records" draws every valid record as a dot (green inside the boundary, red outside) over the boundary preview or the results, so you can check that the coordinates and the boundary line up before running; more than 20,000 records are thinned for display. "Basemap" adds a graticule (longitude/latitude lines) and Natural Earth 1:110m country outlines (public domain), both drawn in the grid projection. Basemaps and records appear in the PNG and SVG exports.

Similarity tab. Pairwise Jaccard and Sorensen similarity, Sorensen dissimilarity, Simpson turnover and the nestedness component between regions or between the occupied cells of one grid, plus Whittaker's beta and the multiple-site Sorensen partition (Baselga 2010) across all units. Multiple-site values depend partly on the number of units, so compare them only between sets with similar numbers of occupied units (not, for instance, the 10 km grid against the 50 km grid). All indices are computed from presence/absence, so no abundance-based index (such as Shannon) is offered: occurrence records do not measure abundance.

Map colours. Choose a palette (Viridis, Magma, Plasma and Cividis are perceptually uniform and colour-blind safe; Yellow-Green, Yellow-Orange-Red, Blues, Greens and Greys are single-hue ColorBrewer schemes that print well), and a classification: continuous colours, or 3 to 9 classes by equal intervals, quantiles (equal numbers of cells per class) or natural breaks (Jenks; groups similar values, usually the best default for skewed richness data). "Reverse" flips the palette, "Cell borders" toggles the white cell outlines. The legend and the exported PNG and SVG follow these settings.

Regions. If the boundary file contains several polygons (divisions, districts, protected areas), tick "Also summarise by region" and choose the field that holds their names. Each region then gets its own record count, richness, records and species per 1000 km2, optional species list and rarefied richness, a map layer ("Regions" in the Grid list), a table (Regions tab), a sheet in the Excel export and a GeoJSON download. The grid is still built over the merged outline.

Charts. The Charts tab explains the results in plain language and draws: richness against records per cell (how much of the pattern is sampling effort), the distribution of richness, the effect of cell size, the most widespread species, and richness and effort by region. Hover any bar or dot for its values.

Interpretation. Observed richness rises with the number of records and with cell size. Compare richness with n_occ, or use rarefied richness, before describing a cell as exceptionally rich; Gridrich reports highest observed richness, not biodiversity hotspots in the sense of a defined hotspot methodology. Grey cells have no records; they are unsampled, not species poor.

Example data. "Load example data" (top right) loads the boundary "Bangladesh" and the records "Aves": the eight divisions of Bangladesh, dissolved from the 64 districts of the geoBoundaries open database (Runfola et al. 2020; source Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics and OCHA ROAP, CC BY 3.0 IGO; simplified to 100 m), and 130,778 bird occurrence records from a GBIF occurrence download (key 0008757-260921141020460, 26 September 2026; 643,016 rows, chiefly eBird checklists published by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, plus iNaturalist and museum specimens). Rows without coordinates, with GBIF coordinate issues, identified only to genus or with coordinate uncertainty above 10 km were removed, coordinate duplicates (same species and coordinates) were collapsed to one row per species and site, and only records published under CC BY 4.0 or CC0 are shipped; 677 species remain. Attribution for the records belongs to their publishers. Because a whole checklist shares one coordinate, record counts here mean the number of species-site combinations, not the number of individual observations. The example files were derived by Kakon Chakma from these sources; the upstream licences and attribution apply.

Validation. Every value of the built-in example (cell counts, areas, records, richness, rarefied richness, densities, species frequencies, presence/absence, Jaccard, Sorensen, Simpson, nestedness, Whittaker and multiple-site indices, for square and hexagonal grids) is recomputed by an independent R script using sf and compared unrounded; the comparison report and the scripts ship with the source.
